\subsection*{1.1}
\paragraph{Proof.}
Suppose it is. Then by the definition of properness there is a sequence of
totally mixed profiles $\sigma^n \to (A,V)$ and reals $\varepsilon_n \to 0$,
$\varepsilon_n > 0$, such that for each player $i$ and every pair of pure
strategies $s,s'$ of that player,
\begin{equation}\tag{$\ast$}
u_i(s,\sigma^n_{-i}) < u_i(s',\sigma^n_{-i})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sigma^n_i(s) \le \varepsilon_n\,\sigma^n_i(s').
\end{equation}
I  write player 1's tremble weights as
$(a_n,b_n,c_n,d_n) = \big(\sigma^n_1(A),\sigma^n_1(B),\sigma^n_1(C),\sigma^n_1(D)\big)$
and player 2's as
$(v_n,w_n,x_n,y_n,z_n) = \big(\sigma^n_2(V),\dots,\sigma^n_2(Z)\big)$.
Being totally mixed, all of these are strictly positive; and
$\sigma^n \to (A,V)$ means $a_n,v_n \to 1$ while the other six weights
$\to 0$.
 
The idea is to use the two player's constraints against each
other. I do it in three steps.
 
\medskip
\textbf{1.}
Against player 1's trembles, player 2's payoffs from $W$, $X$ and $Y$ are
\[
u_2(W,\sigma^n_1) = a_n + d_n,\qquad
u_2(X,\sigma^n_1) = a_n + c_n,\qquad
u_2(Y,\sigma^n_1) = 0,
\]
because column $Y$ gives player 2 zero in every row, while $W$ and $X$ each
give him a $1$ in row $A$. Since $a_n \to 1$, for $n$ large both $a_n+d_n$ and
$a_n+c_n$ are strictly positive, so $Y$ is strictly worse than $W$ and than $X$.
By $(\ast)$,
\[
y_n \le \varepsilon_n\, w_n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y_n \le \varepsilon_n\, x_n .
\]
In particular $y_n < w_n$ and $y_n < x_n$ for $n$ large (eventually
$\varepsilon_n < 1$).
 
\textbf{2.}
For player 1, against player 2's trembles\footnote{I suppres the superscript $n$ for
readability}:
\[
u_1(B) = v+2w+2x+z,\quad
u_1(C) = v+w+2x+y+z,\quad
u_1(D) = v+2w+x+y+z,
\]
so, cancelling, the only columns that survive are $W,X,Y$ and
\[
u_1(B) - u_1(C) = w_n - y_n,\qquad
u_1(B) - u_1(D) = x_n - y_n .
\]
By Step 1 both differences are strictly positive for $n$ large, so $C$ and $D$
are each strictly worse than $B$. Applying $(\ast)$ to player 1,
\[
c_n \le \varepsilon_n\, b_n,\qquad
d_n \le \varepsilon_n\, b_n,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
c_n + d_n \le 2\varepsilon_n\, b_n < b_n
\]
once $\varepsilon_n < \tfrac12$.

\textbf{3}
Finally, step 3: 

Now we compare columns $V$ and $Z$ for player 2:
\[
u_2(V,\sigma^n_1) = a_n + b_n + c_n + d_n = 1,
\qquad
u_2(Z,\sigma^n_1) = a_n + 2b_n,
\]
so
\[
u_2(V,\sigma^n_1) - u_2(Z,\sigma^n_1) = (c_n + d_n) - b_n < 0
\]
for $n$ large, by 2. Thus $V$ is strictly worse than $Z$, and $(\ast)$
gives
\[
v_n \le \varepsilon_n\, z_n \le \varepsilon_n \to 0 .
\]
But $\sigma^n \to (A,V)$ forces $v_n \to 1$. Contradiction. \qquad$\blacksquare$

\subsection*{1.1}
\paragraph{Proof.}
Suppose it is. Then by the definition of properness there is a sequence of
totally mixed profiles $\sigma^n \to (A,V)$ and reals $\varepsilon_n \to 0$,
$\varepsilon_n > 0$, such that for each player $i$ and every pair of pure
strategies $s,s'$ of that player,
\begin{equation}\tag{$\ast$}
u_i(s,\sigma^n_{-i}) < u_i(s',\sigma^n_{-i})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sigma^n_i(s) \le \varepsilon_n\,\sigma^n_i(s').
\end{equation}
I  write player 1's tremble weights as
$(a_n,b_n,c_n,d_n) = \big(\sigma^n_1(A),\sigma^n_1(B),\sigma^n_1(C),\sigma^n_1(D)\big)$
and player 2's as
$(v_n,w_n,x_n,y_n,z_n) = \big(\sigma^n_2(V),\dots,\sigma^n_2(Z)\big)$.
Being totally mixed, all of these are strictly positive; and
$\sigma^n \to (A,V)$ means $a_n,v_n \to 1$ while the other six weights
$\to 0$.
 
The idea is to use the two player's constraints against each
other. I do it in three steps.
 
\medskip
\textbf{1.}
Against player 1's trembles, player 2's payoffs from $W$, $X$ and $Y$ are
\[
u_2(W,\sigma^n_1) = a_n + d_n,\qquad
u_2(X,\sigma^n_1) = a_n + c_n,\qquad
u_2(Y,\sigma^n_1) = 0,
\]
because column $Y$ gives player 2 zero in every row, while $W$ and $X$ each
give him a $1$ in row $A$. Since $a_n \to 1$, for $n$ large both $a_n+d_n$ and
$a_n+c_n$ are strictly positive, so $Y$ is strictly worse than $W$ and than $X$.
By $(\ast)$,
\[
y_n \le \varepsilon_n\, w_n
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
y_n \le \varepsilon_n\, x_n .
\]
In particular $y_n < w_n$ and $y_n < x_n$ for $n$ large (eventually
$\varepsilon_n < 1$).
 
\textbf{2.}
For player 1, against player 2's trembles\footnote{I suppres the superscript $n$ for
readability}:
\[
u_1(B) = v+2w+2x+z,\quad
u_1(C) = v+w+2x+y+z,\quad
u_1(D) = v+2w+x+y+z,
\]
so, cancelling, the only columns that survive are $W,X,Y$ and
\[
u_1(B) - u_1(C) = w_n - y_n,\qquad
u_1(B) - u_1(D) = x_n - y_n .
\]
By Step 1 both differences are strictly positive for $n$ large, so $C$ and $D$
are each strictly worse than $B$. Applying $(\ast)$ to player 1,
\[
c_n \le \varepsilon_n\, b_n,\qquad
d_n \le \varepsilon_n\, b_n,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
c_n + d_n \le 2\varepsilon_n\, b_n < b_n
\]
once $\varepsilon_n < \tfrac12$.

\textbf{3}
Finally, step 3: 

Now we compare columns $V$ and $Z$ for player 2:
\[
u_2(V,\sigma^n_1) = a_n + b_n + c_n + d_n = 1,
\qquad
u_2(Z,\sigma^n_1) = a_n + 2b_n,
\]
so
\[
u_2(V,\sigma^n_1) - u_2(Z,\sigma^n_1) = (c_n + d_n) - b_n < 0
\]
for $n$ large, by 2. Thus $V$ is strictly worse than $Z$, and $(\ast)$
gives
\[
v_n \le \varepsilon_n\, z_n \le \varepsilon_n \to 0 .
\]
But $\sigma^n \to (A,V)$ forces $v_n \to 1$. Contradiction. \qquad$\blacksquare$
 
\subsection*{1.2}
Basically I need to show that is Sequential Equilibria, but making sure the proof or the argument is applicable to any extensive form game that we may draw. 

Suppose player 2 plays $V$, so by (a) player 1 gets $1$ no matter which row she ends up
playing. Hence at every information set of player 1, every continuation --- $A$
included --- yields $1$, so $A$ is (weakly) optimal there under whatever beliefs
she holds. Nothing about $\Gamma$ entered this; player 1's sequential rationality
is automatic in any tree.

By the first half of (b), $V$ yields a constant $1$ at any information set,
whatever the belief $\mu$. So the only way $V$ could fail to be optimal is if some
other column beats $1$. Writing the belief's marginal over player 1's row as
$(\mu_A,\mu_B,\mu_C,\mu_D)$,
\[
u_2(V) = 1,\quad u_2(W)=\mu_A+\mu_D\le 1,\quad u_2(X)=\mu_A+\mu_C\le 1,\quad
u_2(Y)=0,\quad u_2(Z)=\mu_A+2\mu_B .
\]
Only $Z$ can exceed $1$, and $u_2(Z) = \mu_A + 2\mu_B > 1$ exactly when
$\mu_B > \mu_C + \mu_D$. So $V$ is a best reply at an information set as soon as
\[
\mu_B \;\le\; \mu_C + \mu_D,
\]
i.e.\ as long as the belief doesn't put more weight on the $B$-node than on $C$
and $D$ together.


On the path, player 1 plays $A$ with probability $1$, so Bayes forces $\mu_A = 1$
(hence $\mu_B = 0$) at every on-path information set of player 2 --- and there $V$
is optimal, tying $Z$ at $1$. Off the path we have freedom, subject to $\mu$ being
a limit of Bayes-updates from totally mixed trembles. Take any sequence
$\sigma^n \to (A,V)$ of totally mixed profiles in which player 1's tremble onto
$B$ is of higher order than his trembles onto $C$ and $D$ --- say
$\sigma^n_1(B) = o\big(\sigma^n_1(C)\big)$ and $\sigma^n_1(B) = o\big(\sigma^n_1(D)\big)$.
Then at every information set the conditional weight on the $B$-node vanishes, so
in the limit $\mu_B = 0 \le \mu_C + \mu_D$, and $V$ is optimal everywhere by the
line above. This recipe never mentioned the shape of $\Gamma$, so it produces a
consistent, sequentially rational $(\sigma,\mu)$ in every tree.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
$(\sigma,\mu)$ is a sequential equilibrium of $\Gamma$ with $\sigma = (A,V)$, and
$\Gamma$ was an arbitrary extensive form with this normal form. So $(A,V)$ is part
of a sequential equilibrium in every such extensive form.

Putting the two halves together: $(A,V)$ lies in the intersection of the
sequential-equilibrium profiles taken over all extensive forms, yet (Part~1) it is
not proper. Hence
\[
\text{proper} \;\subsetneq\; \bigcap_{\Gamma}\ \text{sequential eq.\ of }\Gamma,
\]
which is the strict inclusion the notes were pointing at.

\textbf{I think intuition is correct, but proof needs to be finished and more formal.}
